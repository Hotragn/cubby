### Recommendation: **Washington (WAC 110-300)**

Washington's rules give a concrete number or required field for every feature on the list, and the source is the official legislature site:
- **Sleep:** visible infant check **every 15 min** (0291). Massachusetts is equally specific (15 min for all nappers, 7.10(7)(d)).
- **Ratios:** family home cap of **12**, with the second-staff trigger points of **>6 with any child under 2, >8 with any under 3, >10 with any under school age** (0355).
- **Medication log:** **6 required fields**, including side effects (0215(3)(b)).
- **Incidents:** **written report within 24 h**, **department within 24 h** for emergencies, **48 h** max for death or abuse, **parent immediately** (0475).
- **Attendance:** arrival and departure times with a **parent signature at each**, explicit **e-signature requirements**, 10 h/day cap, 5-year retention (0455, 0465).

Massachusetts is a solid second choice (15-min nap checks, 10-child FCC cap, immediate phone report plus 48 h written report to EEC, written note to parents within 24 h). Its weak spots are that it has no general authorized-pickup rule and doesn't specify incident report fields.

## Part C: Market size (home-based child care)

1. **BLS (Occupational Outlook Handbook, childcare workers):** median pay **$34,980/yr or $16.82/hr (May 2025)**, about **1,010,300 jobs (2025)**, about **24% self-employed**. BLS notes the pay figure **excludes self-employed workers**, so it doesn't measure home-provider earnings. https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/childcare-workers.htm
2. **Child Care Aware of America, "Child Care at a Standstill: Price and Landscape Analysis 2023":** licensed FCC homes fell from **107,041 (2019) to 94,227 (2023)** across 39 states with data, a **12% decline**, and about **0.8% fewer from 2022 to 2023**. https://www.childcareaware.org/?p=76062
3. **NSECE 2019 (OPRE Report 2021-85 chartbook):** listed home-based providers fell from **121,000 (2012) to 91,200 (2019)**, and almost **800,000 children** were in their care. **UNVERIFIED**: the figures come from a search snippet only, because acf.gov returned 403 to my fetches. https://acf.gov/sites/default/files/documents/opre/NSECE-chartbook-homebased-may-2021.pdf
4. **ACF Office of Child Care:** more than **97,000 licensed FCC homes closed between 2005 and 2017**. **UNVERIFIED** (search snippet only, page returned 403). https://acf.gov/occ/news/decreasing-number-family-child-care-providers-united-states

I found no verified figure for typical home-provider earnings. The BLS number excludes the self-employed.
